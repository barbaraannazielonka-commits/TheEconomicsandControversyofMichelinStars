Section 01 · History & Purpose

Why Did a Tire Company Start Rating Food?

In 1900, cars were rare. Brothers Édouard and André Michelin made rubber tires in France. They realized a simple business truth: if people drive farther, their tires wear out faster—so they buy more Michelin tires.

Interactive Timeline — Click a year or drag the slider
1900 · France

35,000 Free Booklets for Drivers

France had fewer than 3,000 cars on the road. Édouard and André Michelin printed nearly 35,000 free guidebooks. The book showed maps, tire repair steps, petrol stations, mechanics, and hotels so drivers felt safe taking long trips.

Business goal: More road trips = worn-out tires = higher tire sales.

Section 03 · The Inspection Process

Inspectors eat 250 to 300 meals each year using fake names. They judge only the food on the plate—not the restaurant's furniture, music, or fancy tablecloths.

Industry Debate (2026) — Switch viewpoints

Are There Enough Inspectors Today?

The Concern

By August 2026, the Michelin Guide listed 19,653 restaurants across five continents. Some chefs and an ex-inspector told The Guardian that the team is stretched thin and may go years without re-visiting every listed restaurant.

Critics also ask if taking tourism-board money (for example, over $1 million from states or cities to launch local guides) puts independence at risk.

Michelin's Position

Michelin keeps its exact staff count secret to protect anonymity. However, the company says its inspectors come from more than 30 nationalities and eat 250 to 300 meals each per year.

Michelin also states that its anonymous inspectors work separately from commercial teams and never accept free meals from restaurants.
